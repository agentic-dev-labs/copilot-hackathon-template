# Day 5: Show What You Built

Today everyone gets a short turn to show what they built or learned this week. You do not need
slides or a finished product. A working page, a skill, a useful Copilot CLI or MCP trick, or a
better way of working with AI all count. Keep everything on screen fictional.

## Today's Route

| When        | What happens                                                            |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 09:00-09:30 | Final polish and practice your story.                                   |
| 09:30-10:30 | Presentations, round 1.                                                 |
| 10:30-10:45 | Break.                                                                  |
| 10:45-12:00 | Presentations, round 2.                                                 |
| 12:00-12:30 | Wrap-up: approved tools, who to ask, keeping your work, and next steps. |

## Your Three Minutes

About **two minutes to show** and **one minute** for a question while the next person gets
ready. Use the story you wrote yesterday in your brief:

1. **What I built:** who it is for and the problem it helps with.
2. **Show it:** click through one thing live.
3. **How I checked it:** one check and what you found, including anything that does not work yet.
4. **How I would use it at work:** the next step, or a question for IT.

An honest "this part does not work yet" is better than a polished claim nobody checked.

## Before Your Turn (09:00-09:30)

1. Make one last small improvement only if it is quick. Do not start anything big today.
2. Save, review, commit, and push, so your work is safe on GitHub.
3. Open your page with `/start-website` and close tabs you do not need.
4. Practice once with a neighbor, with a timer.
5. Take one screenshot as a backup. If the live page fails during your turn, show it and say it is a screenshot.
6. Check your screen shows only fictional content: no real names, customers, patients, or work records.

## How the Rounds Work

- The host calls people by table and keeps time, with a signal at 30 seconds left.
- The host explains how to share your screen in the room before round 1.
- Listeners offer one question or one thing they liked.
- With more than about 40 people, pairs or tables present together, so the wrap-up keeps its time.

## Wrap-Up (12:00-12:30)

- Which AI tools are approved for real work, and who to ask before using them with real data.
- Where your work lives: your repository stays available for the agreed period. Ask before
  moving to other tools, and never remove training access or delete an account yourself.
- Stop your Codespace when you finish; closing the browser tab does not stop it.
- What to try next: keep building in your repository, try one of the later ideas in your brief,
  and take the questions for IT you wrote this week to your technical owner.

**Done when:** you have shown what you built or learned, and you know where your work lives and
who to ask next.

## Optional extras

1. Write a five-line summary of what you built and learned for your manager.
2. Note one idea you would like to try next month, and who could help.
